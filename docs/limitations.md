# Limitations

- **Prices and contracts:** Cushing spot and continuous front-month WTI are benchmark proxies. Reconstructing a producer's hedge requires dated contracts, expiry and roll calendars, validated settlements and execution costs. The EIA C1–C4 history ends in April 2024; the carry study excludes that partial month.
- **Physical basis:** The observed Cushing spot-minus-futures spread includes timing and roll effects. Midland–Cushing values remain assumed location stresses; quality, transport and marketing deductions need separate data.
- **Hedge economics:** The base case assumes known monthly production and simplified month-end timing. Production uncertainty is explored separately. Execution, margin, liquidity, funding, credit, tax and hedge-accounting effects are omitted; swaps and futures share terminal payoffs while differing in these costs.
- **Options:** Black-76 assumes positive forwards, European exercise, one terminal settlement and common constant volatility. It excludes smiles, American exercise and averaging. Negative terminal payoffs can be stressed, but negative inception forwards cannot be priced. Simulated revenue distributions are risk-neutral scenarios rather than forecasts; zero premium does not remove the risks created by sold options.
- **Statistics:** Hedge ratios and tail measures depend on the sample, estimation window and stability of the physical/futures relationship. VaR and ES do not bound the worst possible loss. Walk-forward and bootstrap results help assess uncertainty. Curve regimes use a descriptive ±$0.25/bbl convention.
- **Use and controls:** This is public/synthetic portfolio research with no employer or client inputs. Operational use would require trade capture, confirmations, settlement controls, market-data governance, independent valuation, limits and audit trails. The self-contained [storage project](../projects/crude-storage-carry/README.md#limitations) documents its additional physical and timing assumptions.
